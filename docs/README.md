# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This folder is the index for OctoAcme's project management processes. The documentation describes a customer-first, iterative, and data-informed lifecycle that helps teams align on outcomes, plan work, deliver safely, and improve continuously.

## Project management process summary

OctoAcme begins with **project initiation**, validating the business need, defining measurable outcomes, identifying stakeholders, and making a go/no-go decision before planning. During **planning**, the team turns the approved initiative into a prioritized backlog, breaks work into shippable increments, estimates scope, defines the Definition of Done, maps dependencies, and agrees on milestones and release plans.

During **execution and tracking**, delivery teams use a project board to move work from backlog through development, review, QA, and completion. Small pull requests, acceptance criteria, automated tests, linting, security scans, code review, and manual QA where needed provide quality controls. Progress is monitored through standups, delivery syncs, demos, velocity or burndown, dashboards, and product success metrics. The PM coordinates delivery while the Product Manager owns outcomes and prioritization; developers, QA, and stakeholders contribute implementation, validation, and decisions.

**Risk management and communication** run throughout the lifecycle. Teams maintain a risk register with owners, impact, likelihood, mitigations, and status, review it regularly, and escalate blockers from the delivery team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor as appropriate. Communication uses regular team and stakeholder updates with a single source of truth for status. **Release and deployment** require completed acceptance criteria, passing CI and security checks, release notes, smoke tests, and a rollback or mitigation plan, followed by staging checks, production verification, and stakeholder notification.

After each sprint, release, milestone, or incident, OctoAcme conducts a **retrospective** to capture what went well, identify improvements, assign a small number of owned action items, and follow up on their impact. This creates a repeatable lifecycle of initiation, planning, execution, release, and learning while promoting transparency, psychological safety, customer value, and continuous improvement.
